# Cornerz Puzzle Evaluation Rubric

Use this rubric to evaluate and compare puzzles before release. Score each puzzle out of 100 points.

If the puzzle has been playtested, let observed player reactions adjust your Aha Value and Difficulty scores; on-paper scores can miss how a puzzle actually feels to solve.

## Structural Requirements

These are release requirements rather than scored criteria. A puzzle must have:

- Exactly 4 categories with 4 words each.
- Exactly 12 unique words.
- Exactly 4 corner words, each belonging to its two intended intersecting categories.
- A valid arrangement around the Cornerz board.
- No duplicate answers, factual errors, or unresolved spelling errors.

A puzzle that fails a structural requirement should be corrected before it is scored.

## Scoring Criteria

### 1. Category Precision and Fairness — 25 points

Each category must have one clear, defensible relationship. Connections should not depend on vague associations, obscure interpretations, or inconsistent rules.

Most importantly, every non-corner word must fit only its intended category. A word fitting another category is undesirable ambiguity, not an intentional misdirection. Corner words are the only words that should belong to two categories, and they should belong only to their two intended intersecting categories.

Award full points when:

- Every non-corner word fits exactly one category.
- Every corner word fits exactly its two intended categories.
- Category rules are specific, consistent, and easy to defend after the solution is revealed.
- No plausible unintended category assignments exist.

Apply a substantial penalty for each unintended cross-category fit. If an unintended fit also creates a second valid solution, apply the penalty here only — do not deduct separately under Unique Solvability for the same word. If the ambiguity makes the intended placement genuinely unsolvable, the puzzle should be revised before release.

### 2. Corner-Word Quality — 20 points

Each corner word should fit both intersecting categories naturally and strongly. Neither connection should feel forced merely to make the geometry work.

### 3. Unique Solvability — 10 points

The intended solution should be the only valid solution. Accidental categories, interchangeable words, or multiple valid board arrangements reduce the score. Note that multiple-solution problems often originate from precision failures already penalized under Criterion 1 — score here only for solvability issues that are not already captured there (e.g., two words that are interchangeable between categories even though both are individually unambiguous).

### 4. Difficulty Balance and Progression — 10 points

The puzzle should give players an entry point — at least one category that is approachable without being a giveaway. A puzzle does not need to span the full difficulty range; accessible puzzles are a valid design goal. Penalize puzzles where every category is equally opaque (no foothold) or where the difficulty ordering actively misleads players about which lines to attempt first.

### 5. "Aha" Value — 15 points

The harder connections should feel satisfying once revealed. Clever wordplay, precise secondary meanings, and surprising but fair connections score well; arbitrary trivia does not. This is the criterion most sensitive to playtest feedback — if players consistently feel a connection is cheap or obscure rather than clever, reduce the score accordingly.

### 6. Misdirection Quality — 5 points

Award points when non-corner words create plausible but ultimately incorrect category associations. A word that looks like it belongs to a different category — until you see why it doesn't — adds tension and makes the solve feel earned. Distinguish this from unintended ambiguity: good misdirection is intentional and survives scrutiny after the solution is revealed; ambiguity does not. Award 0 if there is no misdirection, 2–3 if one word creates a meaningful false lead, 4–5 if multiple words do so in a way that feels designed rather than accidental.

### 7. Word and Category Diversity — 5 points

Categories should use varied mechanisms and subject areas. Repeating the same construction—such as several "words that follow ___" categories—makes a puzzle feel less distinctive.

### 8. Familiarity and Accessibility — 5 points

Most clues should be recognizable to the intended audience. Specialized knowledge is acceptable in moderation, especially when the remaining words make the answer reasonably inferable.

### 9. Editorial Polish — 5 points

Spelling, capitalization, category labels, factual accuracy, and solution explanations should be clean and consistent.

## Rating Bands

| Score | Rating |
|---:|---|
| 90–100 | Excellent |
| 80–89 | Good |
| 70–79 | Solid, but needs refinement |
| 60–69 | Weak |
| Below 60 | Rework or remove |

## Release Flags

Record these separately from the numerical score because they can prevent release even when the overall score is acceptable:

- A non-corner word fits more than one category.
- A corner word fits a third category.
- More than one valid solution or board arrangement exists.
- A connection depends on a factual error.
- A spelling or wording problem changes how a clue can be interpreted.
- A category cannot be explained with one consistent rule.
